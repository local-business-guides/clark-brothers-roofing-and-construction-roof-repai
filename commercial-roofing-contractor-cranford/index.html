Scope, schedule, and terms to know

Commercial roofing projects vary in size and detail. A contractor may discuss the roof’s condition, the materials involved, the work area, and the sequence for completing the project. Timing depends on the scope, access, material needs, and conditions during the work. Cost is generally shaped by the work required, materials, and any additional tasks. Request a written proposal that identifies included work, exclusions, and the expected schedule before making a decision.

Useful points to confirm include:
- What parts of the roof will be inspected and what the findings show.
- Which tasks, materials, and preparation are included in the scope.
- How the work may affect access to the building or its daily use.
- What factors could change the schedule or the proposed work.

When comparing providers, consider how specifically each proposal describes the roof work. Ask each contractor to explain the basis for its recommendation, how related issues will be handled, and what is not included. Compare the plans and written scopes, not just a brief summary. Common terms include “membrane,” a flexible roofing layer used on some low-slope roofs; “flashing,” material that helps protect joints and openings; and “drainage,” the way water is directed off the roof.
